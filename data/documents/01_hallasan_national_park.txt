Title: Hallasan National Park Visitor and Hiking Guide
Source: Jeju Tourism Organization & Hallasan National Park Authority
Access Date: 2026-10-15
Category: Natural Heritage & Mountain Trails

Overview:
Hallasan Mountain stands in the center of Jeju Island as the highest peak in South Korea, reaching an altitude of 1,947 meters (6,388 feet) above sea level. It is a shield volcano formed during the Cenozoic Era and is designated as UNESCO World Natural Heritage, Biosphere Reserve, and Global Geopark. The mountain is home to over 1,800 species of flora and over 4,000 species of fauna, including diverse alpine ecosystems and the largest Korean fir (Abies koreana) forest in the world.

Hiking Trails and Summit Access:
Hallasan National Park features five official hiking routes, but only two routes provide access to the Baeknokdam crater summit:
1. Seongpanak Trail:
   - Distance: 9.6 km one-way (approx. 4.5 to 5 hours up).
   - Difficulty: Moderate, with steady wooden stairs, basalt rock paths, and boardwalks.
   - Facilities: Jindallaebat Shelter (rest area and check point).
   - Summit reservation: Strictly mandatory through the Hallasan Trail Reservation System.
2. Gwaneumsa Trail:
   - Distance: 8.7 km one-way (approx. 5 hours up).
   - Difficulty: Strenuous and steep, featuring deep valleys, dramatic suspension bridges, and panoramic cliff vistas.
   - Facilities: Tamna Valley and Samgakbong Shelter.
   - Summit reservation: Strictly mandatory through the official reservation portal.

Non-Summit Day Trails:
3. Yeongsil Trail (5.8 km, 2.5 hours): Known for the dramatic Yeongsil Giam rocks and alpine meadows leading to Witseoreum Shelter (1,700m). Does not reach Baeknokdam summit. No reservation required.
4. Eorimok Trail (6.8 km, 3 hours): Passes through Sajebi Hill and Mansaedongsan toward Witseoreum Shelter. Ideal for autumn foliage and winter snowscapes. No reservation required.
5. Donnaeko Trail (7.0 km, 3.5 hours): Climbs from Seogwipo on the southern flank to the South Wall Junction (Nambyeok Bungijeom).

Visitor Rules, Checkpoints, and Seasonal Cutoff Times:
- Strict Entry Cutoff: Hikers must pass designated control shelters before midday (e.g., Jindallaebat cutoff is 12:00 PM in winter, 12:30 PM in spring/autumn, and 1:00 PM in summer) to reach the summit and descend safely before dusk.
- Mandatory Gear: Trekking boots with ankle support are required. Crampons (spikes) and trekking poles are mandatory during the winter snow season (December to March).
- Zero-Waste Policy: All trash must be carried back down. Smoking, cooking, open flames, and unauthorized camping are strictly prohibited and subject to heavy fines under the Natural Parks Act.
- Admission Fee: Free admission. Parking fees apply at trailheads (1,800 KRW for standard passenger cars).
