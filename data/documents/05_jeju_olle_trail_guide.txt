Title: Jeju Olle Trail Trekking Guide and Trail Etiquette
Source: Jeju Olle Foundation & Jeju Special Self-Governing Province
Access Date: 2026-10-15
Category: Walking Trails, Eco-Tourism & Cultural Immersion

Trail Concept and Network:
"Olle" is a native Jeju dialect term referring to the narrow, winding stone path connecting a private house gate to the village road. Founded in 2007 by journalist Suh Myung-sook, the Jeju Olle Trail is an internationally celebrated long-distance trekking network encircling the entire perimeter of Jeju Island, including adjacent inhabited islands.
- Total Scale: 27 official walking routes comprising 21 main routes and 6 sub-routes (such as Route 1-1 on Udo Island and Route 10-1 on Gapado Island).
- Total Distance: Approximately 437 kilometers.
- Wayfinding Markers:
  - Ganse: A stylized pony sign inspired by Jeju's indigenous pony (Jorangmal); the head always points toward the direction of forward travel.
  - Ribbons: Two intertwined strips of vibrant blue (symbolizing the Jeju sea) and orange (symbolizing Jeju tangerines) tied to trees and posts every 30 to 50 meters.
  - Directional Arrows: Blue arrows painted on asphalt, stones, and fences indicate the standard forward route; orange arrows signify reverse route direction.

Popular Olle Routes:
1. Route 01 (Siheung to Gwangchigi, 15.1 km, 4-5 hours): The inaugural trail winding through green carrot fields, climbing Malmioreum for panoramic coastal views, and concluding at Gwangchigi Beach facing Seongsan Ilchulbong.
2. Route 07 (Jeju Olle Tourist Center to Wolpyeong, 17.6 km, 5-6 hours): Regarded as one of the most picturesque coastal segments, featuring the sea-stack rock Oedolgae, Beopwhan port where female divers work, and the subtropical Gangjeong stream.
3. Route 10 (Hwasun to Moseulpo, 15.6 km, 5 hours): Passes Sanbangsan volcano, Yongmeori Coast, Songaksan crater cliffs, and coastal Japanese WWII military tunnels.

Olle Passport and Stamping System:
- Walkers can purchase an official Olle Passport at Jeju Olle Information Centers (20,000 KRW for a two-volume set).
- Each route has three unique commemorative stamps: Start point, Midpoint, and Finish point.
- Hikers completing all 27 routes earn an official completion certificate and medal from the Jeju Olle Foundation.

Safety Guidelines and Trail Etiquette:
- Trekking Hours: Hiking is strictly recommended between sunrise and sunset. Walking after dusk is discouraged due to unlit coastal and forest paths.
- Emergency Contact: The Jeju Olle Call Center operates at 064-762-2190. For immediate police assistance, dial 112.
- Trail Etiquette: Respect private farmland, do not pick crops or tangerines without permission, keep dogs on a leash, and pack out all personal waste.
