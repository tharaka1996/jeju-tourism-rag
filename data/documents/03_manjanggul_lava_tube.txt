Title: Manjanggul Lava Tube Geological Visitor Guide
Source: Jeju World Natural Heritage Center
Access Date: 2026-10-15
Category: Subterranean Volcanic Caves & Geology

Geological Background:
Manjanggul Cave is one of the finest lava tunnels in the world and forms part of the renowned Geomunoreum Lava Tube System. Formed approximately 200,000 to 300,000 years ago when basaltic lava erupted from the Geomunoreum parasitic volcano and flowed toward the coast, the subterranean conduit developed as the outer lava cooled and hardened while inner molten lava continued to drain out. The total surveyed length of Manjanggul Cave is approximately 7.4 km, with ceiling heights ranging from 2 meters to 23 meters and tunnel widths up to 18 meters. A 1.0 km section (Entrance No. 2 to the Great Lava Column) is open for public visitation.

Key Cave Formations and Sights:
- Lava Column (Stone Pillar): At the terminus of the 1 km visitor walkway stands an awe-inspiring 7.6-meter-high lava column, recognized as the largest known lava column in the world. It formed when molten lava cascading through a ceiling skylight pooled and solidified.
- Turtle Rock (Geobukbawi): A massive lava raft shaped strikingly like Jeju Island and a sea turtle, formed when detached ceiling basalt fell into moving lava and was coated by subsequent lava flows.
- Lava Flow Lines and Benches: Distinct horizontal striations etched along cave walls mark fluctuating liquid lava levels during eruption pulses.
- Cave Ecosystem: Manjanggul shelters a delicate micro-ecosystem including the rare bent-winged bat (Miniopterus schreibersii fuliginosus), cave spiders, and subterranean insects. To protect habitat integrity, the non-public 6.4 km sections remain strictly sealed.

Practical Visitor Advice:
- Internal Microclimate: The temperature inside the lava tube remains constantly between 11°C and 15°C (52°F-59°F) year-round with relative humidity exceeding 85%. Visitors should wear a warm windbreaker or light jacket, even during the peak of midsummer.
- Footwear: The cave floor consists of uneven volcanic pahoehoe lava, jagged basalt surfaces, and natural dripping water puddles. High heels, flip-flops, and slick slippers are strictly discouraged; sturdy walking shoes or sneakers are required.
- Operating Schedule: 09:00 to 18:00 (Last entry at 17:10). Closed on the first Wednesday of every month for heritage preservation inspections.
- Admission Fees: Adults 4,000 KRW; Youth/Children 2,000 KRW.
